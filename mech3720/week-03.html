The First Law for Control Volumes

Real engineering devices have fluid streaming through them. Switching from a closed system to a control volume adds two ideas: mass can now cross the boundary, and pushing that mass in and out is itself a form of work. Absorb that flow work into enthalpy and the energy balance becomes the single most useful equation in the course.

Conservation of mass

The rate at which mass piles up inside a control volume equals what flows in minus what flows out:

$$\frac{dm_{cv}}{dt} = \sum_i \dot m_i - \sum_e \dot m_e \qquad \dot m = \rho A\mathrm{v} = \frac{A\mathrm{v}}{v}$$

The 1-D mass-flow rate is density × area × velocity. Mind the notation clash: $\mathrm{v}$ (roman) is velocity, $v$ (italic) is specific volume. At steady state the accumulation term vanishes and $\sum \dot m_i = \sum \dot m_e$.

Flow work and the energy balance

To push a parcel of fluid across the boundary against the local pressure takes work $p v$ per unit mass. Grouping that with internal energy gives enthalpy $h = u + pv$ — which is exactly why enthalpy, not internal energy, rides along with each stream. The full energy rate balance is:

$$\frac{dE_{cv}}{dt} = \dot Q_{cv} - \dot W_{cv} + \sum_i \dot m_i\!\left(h_i + \tfrac{\mathrm{v}_i^2}{2} + gz_i\right) - \sum_e \dot m_e\!\left(h_e + \tfrac{\mathrm{v}_e^2}{2} + gz_e\right)$$

Here $\dot W_{cv}$ is shaft (or other) work only — the flow work is already inside the enthalpies.

The steady-flow energy equation

Almost every device runs at steady state with one inlet and one exit, collapsing the balance to the workhorse form:

$$0 = \dot Q_{cv} - \dot W_{cv} + \dot m\!\left[(h_i - h_e) + \frac{\mathrm{v}_i^2 - \mathrm{v}_e^2}{2} + g(z_i - z_e)\right]$$

Solving a device is then a matter of deciding which terms drop out.

The four steady-flow devices

  • Nozzles & diffusers — no work, usually adiabatic; the enthalpy drop feeds kinetic energy: $h_i - h_e \approx (\mathrm{v}_e^2 - \mathrm{v}_i^2)/2$. A nozzle speeds the flow up, a diffuser slows it down.
  • Turbines, compressors & pumps — the KE, PE and (for an adiabatic machine) heat terms drop, leaving $\dot W_{cv}/\dot m = h_i - h_e$. Positive for a turbine (work out), negative for a compressor or pump (work in).
  • Heat exchangers — no work; energy passes between streams. Taking both streams as the control volume, the enthalpy gained by one equals that lost by the other.
  • Throttling valves — no work, adiabatic, negligible KE/PE, so $h_e \approx h_i$: throttling is essentially isenthalpic. It is how the pressure drop is produced in a refrigeration cycle.

Transient analysis

Filling or emptying a tank keeps the accumulation terms. Integrating the mass and energy balances over the process (with uniform inlet/exit states) gives the transient forms — algebraic relations for the mass and internal energy in the tank before and after charging or discharging.
