Heat Exchangers

The capstone application of the conduction and convection weeks. A heat exchanger transfers heat between two fluids separated by a wall. This week covers the configurations, the overall coefficient with fouling, and the two analysis methods — LMTD for sizing, and effectiveness–NTU for rating.

Configurations

The main families are concentric-tube (parallel-flow or counterflow), cross-flow (with each fluid mixed or unmixed), shell-and-tube (with multiple shell and tube passes), and compact cores for high area density. Counterflow is thermodynamically the best arrangement — it maintains the most uniform driving difference and can push the cold outlet above the hot outlet.

Overall coefficient & fouling

The wall between the fluids is a resistance network: inner convection, wall conduction, outer convection, plus $1/UA = \sum R_t$. Over time deposits add a fouling resistance $R''_f$, and fins add through the overall surface efficiency $\eta_o$:

$$\frac{1}{UA} = \frac{1}{(\eta_o hA)_c} + \frac{R''_{f,c}}{(\eta_o A)_c} + R_w + \frac{R''_{f,h}}{(\eta_o A)_h} + \frac{1}{(\eta_o hA)_h}$$

Note $UA$ is symmetric ($U_cA_c = U_hA_h$), but $U$ alone must be tied to a stated area.

The LMTD method (sizing)

An energy balance gives the heat rate from the capacity rates $q = C_h\Delta T_h = C_c\Delta T_c$, and the transfer relation uses the log-mean temperature difference:

$$q = UA\,\Delta T_{lm}, \qquad \Delta T_{lm} = \frac{\Delta T_2 - \Delta T_1}{\ln(\Delta T_2/\Delta T_1)}$$

The end differences $\Delta T_1,\Delta T_2$ are defined by the arrangement. For multipass and cross-flow units the counterflow LMTD is scaled by a chart correction factor $F < 1$. This method is ideal for design: known duty and temperatures → find the required area $A$.

The effectiveness–NTU method (rating)

When the exchanger size is known but the outlet temperatures are not, the LMTD method needs iteration. The ε–NTU method avoids it. Define the effectiveness and the number of transfer units:

$$\varepsilon = \frac{q}{q_{max}}, \quad q_{max} = C_{min}(T_{h,i}-T_{c,i}), \qquad NTU = \frac{UA}{C_{min}}, \quad C_r = \frac{C_{min}}{C_{max}}$$

For each configuration $\varepsilon = f(NTU, C_r)$ is given as a relation or chart, so the heat rate and outlets follow directly — ideal for rating an existing unit.

Which method? LMTD → you know the temperatures and want the size. ε–NTU → you know the size and want the performance. Both use the same $UA$.
