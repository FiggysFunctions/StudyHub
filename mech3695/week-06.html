Internal Convection

Flow inside pipes and ducts — the heat transfer that happens in every heat exchanger. The new feature versus external flow is that the fluid is confined: there is no fixed free stream, so we track a mean temperature that changes along the tube, and the flow develops toward a fully developed state.

Developing & fully developed flow

Entering a tube, both the velocity and temperature profiles develop over an entrance length before settling into a fixed shape. For laminar flow $x_{fd,h}/D \approx 0.05\,Re_D$, with the thermal length longer by a factor $Pr$. Transition is at $Re_D \approx 2300$, using the mean velocity $u_m = \dot m/\rho A_c$. Non-circular ducts reuse circular correlations via the hydraulic diameter $D_h = 4A_c/P$.

The mean temperature & energy balance

Internal convection is referenced to the flow-weighted mean temperature $T_m$, so $q'' = h(T_s - T_m)$ — but unlike $T_\infty$, $T_m$ rises along the tube as heat is added. An energy balance on a slice, $q'' P\,dx = \dot m c_p\,dT_m$, gives the axial temperature for the two canonical wall conditions.

Constant heat flux

With a uniform wall flux (e.g. electrical heating), $T_m$ rises linearly and, once fully developed, $T_s$ runs parallel to it:

$$T_m(x) = T_{m,i} + \frac{q''_s P}{\dot m c_p}\,x$$

Constant surface temperature

With a fixed wall temperature the difference decays exponentially:

$$\frac{T_s - T_m(x)}{T_s - T_{m,i}} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{P\bar h}{\dot m c_p}x\right)$$

Integrating over the tube gives the heat rate in terms of the log-mean temperature difference $q = \bar h A_s\,\Delta T_{lm}$ — the same LMTD that governs heat exchangers next week. When the outside sees a fixed external fluid, the group $P\bar h$ is replaced by $1/R_{tot}$ using the overall $U$.

Nusselt correlations

For fully developed laminar flow the Nusselt number is a constant — $Nu_D = 4.36$ (constant flux) or $3.66$ (constant $T_s$) — independent of flow rate. For turbulent flow the workhorse is Dittus–Boelter:

$$Nu_D = 0.023\,Re_D^{4/5}Pr^{n}, \qquad n = 0.4\ (\text{heating}),\ 0.3\ (\text{cooling})$$

Heat transfer is enhanced by raising $h$ (roughened walls, twisted-tape inserts, coiling) or the area $A_s$ (fins).

Where this leads Everything here — $U$, the LMTD, and internal $h$ from Dittus–Boelter — is exactly what sizes a heat exchanger in Week 7.
