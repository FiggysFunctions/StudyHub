Electrical & Electromechanical Systems

The third domain, dropped straight into last week's framework. Nothing new is required except identifying which variables play the roles of effort, flow, momentum and displacement — then the CCRs, state counting and modelling procedure all carry over unchanged.

Slotting electrical into the framework

The power variables are the familiar pair, and the energy variables follow by integration:

$$\text{Effort} \to V(t) \qquad \text{Flow} \to I(t) \qquad \text{Momentum} \to \lambda(t) \qquad \text{Displacement} \to q(t)$$

Charge $q$ is the displacement (current is its derivative, $I = \dot q$) and magnetic linked flux $\lambda$ is the momentum. So the states are the linked flux in inductors and the charge in capacitors.

State counting Unlike mechanical systems there is no "twice the DOF" rule. The number of states equals the number of independent energy-storing elements — capacitors and inductors. Capacitors in parallel or inductors in series are dependent and count once.

The three basic components

Each is one of the archetypes from Week 2:

$$\underbrace{\lambda = LI,\ \ V = \dot\lambda}_{\text{inductor (inertia)}} \qquad \underbrace{q = CV,\ \ I = \dot q}_{\text{capacitor}} \qquad \underbrace{V = RI}_{\text{resistor}}$$

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field; because field and current are co-dependent neither can change instantly, which is what lets inductors smooth current. A capacitor stores energy in an electric field between plates. A resistor dissipates it as heat.

Symbol collision Watch out in multi-domain problems: $L$ is angular momentum in rotational mechanics and inductance here. Likewise $C$ is capacitance and compliance. Define your symbols.

Kirchhoff's laws are the SSRs

Where mechanical systems used free-body diagrams, electrical systems use Kirchhoff:

$$\sum_{\text{node}} I = 0 \qquad \sum_{\text{loop}} V = 0$$

KCL says charge does not accumulate at a node; KVL says potential differences around a closed loop sum to zero. These supply exactly the relations needed to eliminate non-state variables from the state equations.

Motors and generators

A permanent-magnet DC motor couples the electrical and rotational domains. Current through the armature induces a field that interacts with the stator magnet, producing torque; a commutator keeps the torque one-directional. Conversely, rotation induces a back-EMF voltage. The two CCRs are:

$$T(t) = K_T\,I_a(t) \qquad e(t) = K_\omega\,\omega(t)$$

This is a gyrator — effort on one side produces flow-proportional quantities on the other. In SI units $K_T$ and $K_\omega$ have the same magnitude and are often written as a single motor constant $K_m$. A generator is the same device run backwards; the CCRs are unchanged.

A realistic motor model adds the armature resistance $R_a$ and inductance $L_a$ in the electrical loop, so that a KVL around the armature ties the supply voltage, the resistive drop, the inductive term and the back-EMF together — giving one electrical state and one mechanical state.

Sign conventions Motor CCRs come with built-in sign conventions. If a problem fixes the torque direction or the terminal polarity, the other is then determined — you do not get to choose both.
